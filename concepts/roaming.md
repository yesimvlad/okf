# What is Roaming

Roaming is a feature provided by mobile carriers that allows a phone to connect to a foreign network when the user travels outside their home country. The home carrier has agreements with local operators abroad, and the device automatically connects through these agreements.

Roaming requires no setup from the user — it activates automatically when the device detects a foreign network, unless manually disabled.

# How Roaming Works

1. The user travels outside their home country.
2. The device searches for available networks and connects to a partner network of the home carrier.
3. Data, calls, and SMS are routed through the home carrier's agreements.
4. The home carrier bills the user for roaming usage — often at significantly higher rates than domestic plans.

# Types of Roaming Charges

| Charge type | Description |
|---|---|
| Data roaming | Per MB or daily flat fee for mobile data abroad |
| Voice roaming | Per-minute charge for calls made or received abroad |
| SMS roaming | Per-message charge for SMS sent abroad |
| Incoming call roaming | Some carriers charge for receiving calls abroad |

Rates vary significantly by carrier and destination. Check with the home carrier before traveling.

# When Roaming Makes Sense

- Short trips where data usage will be minimal
- Destinations where the home carrier has favorable roaming agreements (e.g. EU roaming for EU residents)
- Users who need seamless call and SMS continuity on the same number

# When Travel eSIM Makes More Sense

- Destinations with high roaming rates
- Longer trips where data consumption is high
- Multi-country trips where roaming costs accumulate
- Users who want a fixed, predictable cost before the trip
- Users who want to install a plan before departure without visiting a store

# EU Roaming

Within the European Union, EU residents can use their home plan in any EU member state at no extra roaming charge (Roam Like At Home regulation). This applies to EU-issued SIM cards used within the EU.

EU roaming does not apply to:
- Travel outside the EU
- Non-EU SIM cards used in the EU
- eSIM plans purchased from non-EU providers

# Travel eSIM is Not Roaming

A travel eSIM plan connects the device directly to a local or regional network through the eSIM provider's agreements — not through the home carrier. The home carrier is not involved in the data connection and does not charge roaming fees for eSIM data usage.

This is a key distinction: using a Yesim eSIM abroad means the user pays Yesim's prepaid rate, not the home carrier's roaming rate.

The physical SIM (home carrier) can remain in the device simultaneously on dual SIM phones for calls and SMS, while the eSIM handles data.

# Common Questions

## What happens if I don't turn off roaming when using a travel eSIM?

On dual SIM devices, if data roaming is enabled on the physical SIM, the device may use the home carrier's roaming connection for some data instead of the eSIM. This can result in unexpected roaming charges. It is recommended to disable data roaming on the physical SIM and set the eSIM as the primary data line when traveling.

## Is a travel eSIM cheaper than roaming?

In most cases, yes — especially for destinations outside the EU or for heavy data users. Exact savings depend on the home carrier's roaming rates and the destination. Compare current plan prices on https://yesim.app before the trip.

## Can I use roaming and a travel eSIM at the same time?

On dual SIM devices, yes. The physical SIM handles home carrier roaming for calls and SMS. The eSIM handles data. Set the eSIM as the preferred data line to avoid accidental data roaming charges on the physical SIM.

## Does a travel eSIM affect my home carrier plan?

No. The eSIM operates as a separate, independent data connection. It does not interact with the home carrier plan, and the home carrier does not bill for eSIM data usage.
